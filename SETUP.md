37. `037_istekler.sql` — İstekler: personel file AVANS (tutar + not) / ŞİKAYET / ÖNERİ requests; finance reviews them from the Personel screen's **İstekler** button (red dot = something waiting). Approving an avans istek calls the existing `give_avans` — identical to Avans Ver (born-`ONAYLANDI` kasa gideri + personel history). Decisions are RPC-only (`approve_avans_istek` / `reject_avans_istek` / `alindi_istek`); no client update path.
38. `038_istek_avans_siniri.sql` — avans isteği cannot exceed the personel's maaş in that business (server-side trigger; no limit when maaş is 0/unset). Personel create istekler from the new **İşlemler** nav tab and track them under **İsteklerim**.
39. `039_komisyon_parent.sql` — links each KK komisyon gider to its parent işlem (`islemler.komisyon_of`); `delete_islem` now removes the komisyon together with its parent (no more orphaned commission in the kasa), and `restore_trash` nulls the link if the parent is gone. Redefines `approve_islem` / `run_daily_materializer` / `delete_islem` / `restore_trash`.
40. `040_onaya_geri_gonder.sql` — **Onaya Geri Gönder** (Yönetici-only): `islem_onaya_geri_gonder` RPC flips an approved işlem back to `BEKLIYOR` (leaves the kasa, reappears on the Onay screen with its stored yöntem/komisyon). The linked KK komisyon gider is removed without hitting the trash and is recreated on re-approval; a cari işlem's hareket returns `YANSIDI` → `BEKLIYOR`. Komisyon rows and `PERSONEL` işlemler (maaş/avans/prim) cannot be sent back. UI: undo-arrow button on Tüm İşlemler cards (Yönetici only) + confirmation pop-up.
41. `041_para_transferi.sql` — **Hesaba Para Aktarımı** (Nakit → Kredi Kartı): `para_transferi(business, tutar)` RPC (finance) writes the transfer as two born-`ONAYLANDI` legs — a NAKİT gider + a KREDİ KARTI gelir linked by `islemler.transfer_of`. The kasa total is unchanged; only the method buckets shift. Transfers are **excluded from gelir/gider totals and reports** (`v_kasa_ozet` + client sums) since they are internal moves, and appear as a **single row** (the sibling leg is hidden client-side). The two legs are inseparable: `delete_islem` on either removes both, transfer rows **never enter the trash** (restoring one leg alone would inflate the kasa), and `islem_onaya_geri_gonder` refuses them. Adds `'TRANSFER'` to the `islem_kaynak` enum.
42. `042_transfer_geri_al.sql` — **Transferi Geri Al**: on TRANSFER rows the undo-arrow button (Yönetici-only) reverses the transfer instead of sending it to Onay. `transfer_geri_al(islem)` does **not** delete — it writes a second, opposite transfer titled "Transfer Geri Alma" (KK gider + Nakit gelir), so the amount returns to Nakit and the history stays intact. `islemler.iade_of` links the reversal to the original with a **partial unique index**, so a transfer can be reversed at most once; a reversal cannot itself be reversed. `delete_islem` refuses to delete a transfer that has been reversed (delete the reversal first).
43. `043_transfer_bakiye_siniri.sql` — **transfer cannot exceed the Nakit balance**: `para_transferi` now reads the business's NAKİT bucket (approved NAKİT gelir − gider, **transfer legs included** — otherwise two back-to-back transfers could spend the same money twice) and refuses any amount above it, serialized per business with a transaction-scoped advisory lock so two concurrent transfers can't both pass on a stale read. The client blocks the keystroke as well (the amount field won't accept a value over the balance, and the label shows "En fazla ₺X"), but this is the real limit. `transfer_geri_al` (042) is deliberately **not** capped — a reversal must always be possible even if the KK bucket was spent in the meantime.
44. `044_onay_yonetici.sql` — **Onay is Yönetici-only** (role change): `approve_islem`, `reject_islem`, `approve_kayit_silme` and `reject_kayit_silme` now require `is_yonetici()` instead of `is_finance()` — Muhasebe keeps every other finance power (creating entries, deleting işlemler, reports, cari, personel) but can no longer *decide* a pending one. The Onay screen and its FAB are hidden for Muhasebe, and `/yonetim/onay` is route-guarded. ONAY/KAYIT_SILME **notifications** move with the permission (new `notify_yonetici` helper + narrowed 031 visibility policy) so Muhasebe isn't pinged about a screen it cannot open; `notify_finance` stays for KAYIT notifications. **Updates ARCHITECTURE.md §3** — "Muhasebe = Yönetici minus role control" is now "minus role control **and** minus Onay".
45. `045_avans_prim_onay.sql` — **avans and prim now go through Onay**: `give_avans` / `give_prim` create the gider born `BEKLIYOR` instead of `ONAYLANDI`, so it reaches the kasa only after the Yönetici approves it on the new **Personel** filter of the Onay screen. **Maaş is unchanged** (`pay_maas` + the cron's automatic salary stay born-`ONAYLANDI`) — a recurring, pre-agreed payment doesn't need re-approving every month. The `personel_odemeler` row is still written immediately, but it is now a *record* rather than a *paid* ledger: Personel Detay shows pending ones with an "Onay bekliyor" badge and **excludes them from the cycle totals** (totals must match the kasa). `reject_islem` deletes the linked `personel_odemeler` row (an advance that was refused shouldn't sit in the employee's ledger), and `delete_islem` now deletes it too — previously the FK's `on delete set null` left an orphan, so an advance deleted from the kasa kept showing under the employee. **Knock-on:** an avans *isteği* approved by Muhasebe (`approve_avans_istek`) now still needs the Yönetici's Onay before the money moves.
46. `046_istekler_yonetici.sql` — **İstekler are Yönetici-only**: avans / şikayet / öneri requests are now invisible to Muhasebe (the `istekler` SELECT policy drops `is_finance` for `is_yonetici` + business access) and the three decision RPCs (`approve_avans_istek`, `reject_avans_istek`, `alindi_istek`) require Yönetici. Visibility is narrowed deliberately, not just the buttons — a şikayet may be *about* the Muhasebe, so leaving the list readable would defeat the feature. The personel side is untouched: staff still file requests and track them under İsteklerim. Client: `/yonetim/istekler` is route-guarded and the İstekler button (with its red dot) is hidden on the Personel roster for Muhasebe.
47. `047_isletme_ayarlari_yonetici.sql` — **İşletme Ayarları is Yönetici-only**: the screen's entire write surface moves from `is_finance` to Yönetici — `businesses` UPDATE (name/telefon/adres + the mesai konum/IP columns from 029) and `kategoriler` INSERT/UPDATE (add + soft-delete). Reads are unchanged: Muhasebe still reads kategoriler (Gelir/Gider Ekle picker, reports) and businesses (İşletme Seç). Client: `/yonetim/ayarlar` sits in the Yönetici-only route block and the FinansMenu item is hidden for Muhasebe — but the boundary is the RLS policies, not the hidden screen. Smoke test PASS 29.
48. `048_izinler.sql` — **İzinler (yıllık izin)**: new `izinler` table (date ranges per member). A person whose range covers today shows an orange "İzinde" badge on the Personel roster, Personel Detay header and Mesai Kayıtları. Write access is **tiered** (enforced in RLS via the `izin_yazabilir` helper, which reads the *target's* role SECURITY DEFINER): Muhasebe manages only PERSONEL members' leave; Yönetici manages everyone's (Muhasebe included). Reads are finance-wide (badges render for all members). Overlapping ranges for the same person are rejected by a trigger (advisory-locked against concurrent inserts); no update path — delete and re-add. Client: "Yıllık İzin" button on Personel Detay → `/yonetim/personel/:id/izinler` (Şu anda aktif / Gelecek / Geçmiş sections, İzin ekle modal). Smoke test PASS 30.
49. `049_onaya_geri_sabit_tekrar.sql` — **onaya geri gönder excludes cron rows**: `islem_onaya_geri_gonder` now also refuses **sabit gider** (`sabit_gider_id`) and **tekrar** (`tekrar_kural_id`) işlemler. These are born `ONAYLANDI` by the cron (016/019) and never pass through Onay, so sending them "back" would drop them into a queue they were designed to skip. The tekrar row can't be told apart by `kaynak` (it is `MANUEL`, same as a real manual entry) — only `tekrar_kural_id` distinguishes it. The client hides the undo-arrow via a shared `onayaGeriGonderilebilir()` selector, but the RPC is the real boundary. Smoke test PASS 31.

With no packets picked, it falls back to a manual amount + free-text açıklama. `give_prim` keeps its signature but now derives the işlem `baslik` from `p_note` (packet names or açıklama, else "Prim"). RLS: write = Yönetici (İşletme Ayarları is already Yönetici-only), read = finance (Muhasebe uses Prim Ver too). **Hard delete** — packets aren't FK-referenced (amount/name are copied onto the işlem), so removing one never touches history. 54. `054_odeme_yontemi_her_yerde.sql` — **ödeme yöntemi on every payment path**. Five flows used to create işlemler with a NULL `odeme_yontemi`, so they moved Toplam Bakiye but landed in no bucket (Nakit + KK + Havale summed to less than the total): maaş, avans, prim, sabit gider, cari tahsilat. The rule now is **the yöntem lives on the definition, never on the approved row**: `business_members.odeme_yontemi` (maaş — edited in Personel Detay's düzenle section, read by both `pay_maas` and the cron's automatic maaş), `sabit_giderler.odeme_yontemi` (edited in the Sabit Gider düzenle modal, copied onto each month's işlem by the materializer), and `cari_hareketler.odeme_yontemi` (chosen when collecting; `yansit_cari_hareket` copies it, so "Tümünü Topla" splits a batch across buckets). Avans/prim take it at creation and the Yönetici can still change it in Onay. `give_avans`/`give_prim`/`pay_maas`/`topla_cari_odeme`/`update_member_pay` gained a `p_yontem` parameter — **old signatures dropped**, since a differing parameter list would create an ambiguous overload rather than replace (same as 033). **Existing rows are deliberately not backfilled and there is no path to change an approved işlem's yöntem**, so `islemler_immutable_guard` is untouched: changing a definition only affects payments made from then on. Client: shared `YontemSecici` used by all five screens.

55. `055_cari_tahsilat_yontemi.sql` — **cari tahsilat yöntemi is picked at collection time**, fixing a gap in 054. 054 stored the yöntem when the hareket was *created*, which is right for "Ödeme Topla" (that flow creates its own hareket) but wrong for **"Borç Ekle"** — recording what someone owes you is not a money movement, and how they will eventually pay is unknown at that moment. The result was that the main cari flow (add debt → "Tümünü Topla" later) produced unbucketed kasa income. Now `yansit_cari_hareket(p_hareket_id, p_yontem)` takes the yöntem at collection: the passed value wins, otherwise it falls back to whatever 054 stored on the hareket (so `topla_cari_odeme` rows are unaffected). The chosen yöntem is also written **back onto the hareket**, so the cari history records how each debt was actually collected. **Old 1-arg signature dropped** (same overload reasoning as 033/054); every existing 1-arg caller — including four in the smoke test — still resolves via the default. Kasa/Onay behaviour is unchanged: still a born-`BEKLIYOR` GELİR, hareket still goes to `BEKLIYOR`. Client: yöntem picker on both the single "Ödeme topla" dialog (pre-filled from the hareket) and the "Tümünü Topla" batch modal (one choice applied to all); the selection resets on every open so a previous choice never silently carries over. Smoke test PASS 37.

56. `056_avans_prim_yontem_zorunlu.sql` — **avans/prim ödeme yöntemi is required at creation** (owner request, reversing the 054/055-era "optional" behaviour). `give_avans` and `give_prim` now raise a Turkish error when `p_yontem` is null, so the rule is enforced server-side rather than by client validation alone. **`give_avans`/`give_prim` signatures are unchanged** (`p_yontem` already existed with a `default null` since 054) — the default is kept deliberately, because removing it would change the signature and risk an ambiguous overload, and a clear `RAISE` message beats a "function does not exist" error. **`approve_avans_istek` gains `p_yontem`** and passes it through: approving a personel's avans isteği *creates* an avans, so it must specify a bucket too — its old 1-arg signature is dropped. Onay is unaffected in behaviour: the yöntem selector was already always open for `PERSONEL`-sourced rows (054), so the Yönetici can approve as-is or change it. Client: pickers on Avans Ver / Prim Ver are now mandatory (no `allowNull`, blocked with "Ödeme yöntemi seçin."), and the İstekler approve step became a form modal with its own picker that resets on each open. Smoke test PASS 36 now asserts the *refusal* of a yöntemsiz avans/prim; every fixture call across PASS 20/23/28/32 passes a yöntem.

57. `057_avans_borcu.sql` — **avans is finally deducted from maaş, and the excess carries forward**. This closes a real defect: the Personel Detay card computed `Kalan (ödenecek) = maaş − avans` and labelled the avans "Maaştan düşülecek", but `pay_maas` created an işlem for the **full** salary — so a ₺15.000 avans followed by Maaş Öde on a ₺30.000 salary took **₺45.000** out of the kasa. Now `pay_maas` **and** the cron's automatic maaş both pay `maaş − borç`, and whatever the salary cannot cover stays outstanding and is deducted from the next period. The debt is **never stored** — it is derived, per the project's "balance is a view, never a stored number" rule: `Σ(approved, borca-sayılan AVANS) − Σ(avans_dusulen on MAAS rows)`, via the internal `avans_borcu()` helper (execute revoked from `authenticated`; the screen computes the same figure from rows it already reads under RLS). Two columns on `personel_odemeler`: `avans_dusulen` (how much of a salary went to the debt — the ledger keeps the **full** salary in `tutar`, so its own `>0` check is untouched) and `borca_sayilir`, which the migration sets to `false` for every pre-existing row — **owner decision: a clean start**, so nobody's next salary is wiped out by historical avans. **Historical over-payments are deliberately not corrected** (no counter-entries, no backfill): the kasa stays low by the previously unrecovered avans and that gap never closes. One constraint change: `islemler.tutar` becomes `tutar > 0 or (tutar = 0 and kaynak = 'PERSONEL')` so a fully-consumed salary can still write a ₺0,00 kasa row; the `> 0` invariant is untouched for kayıt/cari/manuel/transfer/sabit gider. Smoke test PASS 38.

58. `058_avans_borcu_revoke.sql` — **fixes a real hole left by 057**. 057 tried to keep the `avans_borcu()` helper server-side with `revoke execute ... from anon, authenticated`, but that is not enough: PostgreSQL grants `EXECUTE` on every new function to **`PUBLIC`**, and `authenticated` is a member of `PUBLIC`, so any signed-in user could still read any colleague's avans debt. This migration revokes from `public` as well (plus the explicit roles for Supabase's default privileges) and then **verifies** with `has_function_privilege`, raising rather than leaving the hole open silently. `pay_maas` and the cron are unaffected — both are `SECURITY DEFINER` and the owner keeps its privilege regardless. Caught by smoke test PASS 38 (a). **Take the lesson for any future function revoke: `from anon, authenticated` alone never closes access — you must revoke from `public`.**

59. `059_maas_borc_kilidi.sql` — **serialises the maaş debt deduction, and closes the cron functions to clients**. 057 *derives* the avans debt and then does a read-modify-write on it (`avans_borcu()` → `least(maaş, borç)` → insert), with no lock in between. Under READ COMMITTED two overlapping deductions both read the same debt and both subtract it: on a ₺30.000 salary with a ₺15.000 debt the correct sequence pays 15.000 + 30.000 = **45.000**, the raced one pays 15.000 + 15.000 = **30.000** — the employee is **₺15.000 short**, and because `avans_borcu()` ends in `greatest(..., 0)` the raw −15.000 is clamped to zero, so **neither the screen nor the audit log shows any trace**. This is the third instance of the pattern in the project and the only unlocked one (043 `para_transferi` and 053 `cepten_geri_ode` already take one), so both maaş paths now take `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('maas:' || business || ':' || profile))`. Placement matters: in `pay_maas` it goes **after** the authorisation/membership checks (a call that will be refused shouldn't hold a lock) but **before** the `avans_borcu()` read; in the cron it goes at the **top of the salary loop**, ahead of the `not exists (MAAS this month)` dedupe — that check raced the same way. The cron loop also gained `order by profile_id, business_id`, because xact locks are held to commit and the loop therefore *accumulates* them; a deterministic order removes the two-materializer deadlock class (`pay_maas` can never deadlock against it — it takes exactly one lock and never asks for a second). **Function bodies are copied verbatim from 057**; a diff shows only the added lines, and no signature changes, so `create or replace` is correct. **Monthly uniqueness of maaş is deliberately unchanged** — `pay_maas` can still be called twice in a period (a manual payment on top of the automatic one is intended), and with the lock that case is now *financially* correct too: the second call sees a zero debt and pays the full salary. Closing it with a unique index would be a behaviour change and needs an owner decision. **Second, unrelated-looking but same class as 058:** `run_daily_materializer` and `run_saat_transitions` were **executable by anyone signed in**. Neither can carry an `is_finance` guard (the cron has no `auth.uid()`, so a guard would fail every night), which means the grant *is* the only boundary — and the `PUBLIC` grant was still in place, so any user including PERSONEL could trigger the nightly job by hand. Their dedupe guards meant **no money that wasn't already due could be created**, but the privilege boundary was open, so both are now revoked `from public` **and** `from anon, authenticated`, with two `DO` blocks verifying that the revoke took *and* that the cron's own role can still execute them (otherwise the breakage would first appear silently at 00:05). Nothing in the app or CI calls either function. Smoke test PASS 39 asserts the lock is present in both paths, that both use the **same `'maas:'` key** (a differing prefix would mean the locks never collide and the fix dies silently), and that the grants are closed — it does **not** test concurrency, which needs two sessions and cannot run inside this file's single rolled-back transaction.

60. `060_mesai_otomatik_kapat.sql` — **closes forgotten mesai sessions, and warns before doing it**. When staff forgot to check out, the session stayed open forever: it blocked the next day's GİRİŞ (029's alternation guard), it counted as **zero** hours in the report (`personSessions` only sums closed sessions), and after three days it silently vanished from "Şu an mesaide" while still being open in the report. This was designed and deferred back in 030; it ships now. New per-business setting `businesses.mesai_bitis_saati` (**NULL = the whole feature is off for that business**, so nothing changes until a Yönetici sets it — writes are already Yönetici-only via 047's `businesses_update`). Two cron jobs run **every 5 minutes**: `mesai_hatirlatma()` sends the person a push at closing time ("Çıkış yapmayı unuttunuz mu?", deduped to once per day), and `mesai_otomatik_kapat()` closes still-open sessions **5 minutes after closing**. The reminder deliberately fires while they're still on site — someone who has already gone home **cannot** check out, since the RPC rejects anything outside the geofence, so a later reminder would be a dead end. Closing works by **two rules**: (1) a session that started **today and before closing** is closed at the closing time; (2) any session open longer than **14 hours** is closed at `giriş + 14 saat`. Rule 2 exists for night shifts, which are a weekly occurrence here — rule 1 structurally cannot close a session that began *after* closing, and an earlier draft collapsed those to a **zero-minute** session, i.e. it erased a night's work on a regular basis. There is now **no zero-length close at all**: rule 1 only ever runs on sessions starting before closing, so çıkış is always after giriş. Rule 1 uses a **threshold** (`now >= closing + 5min`) rather than a closed time window, so a delayed cron tick self-heals instead of losing the day; it also requires the giriş to be **from today**, without which a stale session would be closed at *today's* closing and fabricate a multi-day session. A person auto-closed while still working simply taps **Giriş** again — that new session starts after closing, so rule 1 leaves it alone, which is also exactly how a night shift begins. Auto-closed sessions **count toward the hour totals** but are flagged `Otomatik kapatıldı` in the report so finance can correct them with the 030 RPCs. Neither function can carry an `is_finance` guard (cron has no `auth.uid()`), so the grant is the only boundary: both are revoked `from public` **and** `from anon, authenticated` with a verifying `DO` block — otherwise any staff member could trigger the close and end their shift at a time of their choosing. Smoke test PASS 40.
61. `061_mesai_gece_bitis.sql` — **superseded by 062; optional to run.** 062 drops everything 061 adds (`if exists`) and recreates both functions in full, so skipping 061 is safe. (While 061's client side sat in the working tree, its `useMesaiKonum` select named `mesai_gece_bitis_saati` and the dev server's Mesai card rendered blank until the column existed — PostgREST rejects the **whole** query rather than skipping an unknown column. That client change never shipped, and 062 removed it.) **Teaches the auto-close what a night shift actually is**. 060's rule 2 was described as "for night shifts", but the system had no concept of one: the only distinction it could make was whether the giriş fell before or after closing. Two consequences. A forgotten real night shift (18:40 → 02:00) was recorded as **18:40 → 08:40**, roughly 6.5 hours too long. And a night worker who clocked in **after midnight** (say 03:00) was classified as *day* — their giriş is before closing — so the evening close caught them. Shrinking the 14-hour cap is not a fix: with an 18:30 closing, a 6-hour cap cuts **every** forgotten day session that started before 12:35 (someone who began at 09:00 would show as ending at 15:00). One duration cannot measure both day and night, so the owner's decision was to **know** the night's end rather than guess it. New per-business setting `businesses.mesai_gece_bitis_saati` (**NULL = the night concept is off**, and 060's behaviour continues unchanged). A DB CHECK enforces `gece_bitis < kapanis`, which makes the two windows partition the day exactly — `gündüz = [gece_bitis, kapanis)` and `gece = [kapanis, 24:00) ∪ [00:00, gece_bitis)`; the reverse ordering would overlap them and every session would be "night", silently killing rule 1. Closing is now **three branches in this order**: (1) giriş today **and in the day window** → close at closing (the added day-window test is what stops the 03:00 night worker from being caught); (2) `gece_bitis` set and giriş **in the night window** → close at the **first `gece_bitis` after the giriş** (18:40 → next day 01:00; 00:30 → same day 01:00), and if that moment has not arrived the session is **left open**, not capped; (3) otherwise the 14-hour cap. **The cap is not removed** — the ordering matters both ways. It must come last, because the night window can stretch to 23.5 hours (a 06:30 closing with a 06:00 night end), and a cap evaluated first would cut a night session in the wrong place. But it must still exist, because a **day session left over from yesterday** fails branch 1 ("giriş today") and is not in the night window, so without the cap it would stay open forever. The reminder (`mesai_hatirlatma`) gains the matching night-window guard, so it only ever reaches people who started in the day window. That guard is **defensive rather than load-bearing**: given `gece_bitis < kapanis`, a post-midnight session is already closed at `gece_bitis` long before the reminder's `now >= kapanis` threshold, so it only matters if the closing cron stalls. **Auto-closing still notifies nobody** — owner decision, and unchanged from 060; the only trace is the `Otomatik kapatıldı` flag in the report. Bodies are copied verbatim from 060 (which is left untouched, per the 055/056 pattern), so the diff is the decision block, the two select lists and the guard. Smoke test PASS 41.
62. `062_mesai_gece_6_saat.sql` — **no ordering constraint with the frontend; 060 must already be live.** This migration *drops* `businesses.mesai_gece_bitis_saati`, but no deployed or current frontend selects that column: 061's client side was never committed — the deployed `bc4198f` selects only 060's `mesai_bitis_saati`, and the working tree has zero references. An earlier version of this note said "deploy the frontend first" on the premise that the live build still read the column; that premise was checked on 2026-09-13 and is false. 062 also does not depend on 061: it drops the constraint and column with `if exists` and recreates both functions in full, so it runs cleanly whether or not 061 ever ran. **Replaces 061's per-business night-end setting with a fixed 6-hour night limit**, at the owner's request ("night shifts are ~6 hours, make it fixed"). The critical detail is that **6 hours applies only to night sessions, not to every session**: the cap is evaluated *before* closing time arrives, so a global 6-hour rule would close a day worker who clocked in at 09:00 at **15:00** — three and a half hours early, while they are still on site — and if they didn't notice and clock in again, the rest of the day would go unrecorded. **A night shift is defined by the hour of the giriş**, so the day is partitioned by wall-clock into **day = `[06:00, closing)`** and **night = `[closing, 23:59] ∪ [00:00, 06:00)`**. Closing is then three branches, and because it is an `if / elsif / elsif` chain **the first match wins** — each branch's real scope only makes sense read in that order: (1) giriş **today and in the day window** → close at closing (**the day shift, unchanged**); (2) giriş **in the night window** → close at `giriş + 6 saat`; (3) everything else → `giriş + 14 saat`. Three flows land in branch 2, all deliberately: someone auto-closed at closing who taps **Giriş** again (18:35 is past the day window, so branch 2 closes it at 00:35); a shift that simply starts in the evening (yesterday 20:00 → 02:00 today, which is what makes the rule self-heal after a stalled cron); and — the reason the `[00:00, 06:00)` half exists — **a night worker who is auto-closed at the 6-hour mark and clocks in again while still working** (00:45 → 06:45). **The `06:00` is derived, not a setting**: `KAPANIS_SAAT_OPTIONS` starts at 06:00, so it is already the app's earliest business hour; the night window has to end *somewhere*, 061 made that a per-business setting and the owner rejected it. If a business closes at 06:00 or earlier the window collapses to `[00:00, closing)`, otherwise the day window would be empty and branch 1 would never fire. **Without the partition** — testing only "is the giriş before closing" — that 00:45 re-entry is calendar-wise "today and before closing", so branch 1 would match it first and drag it to *that evening's* closing: a fabricated ~17.5-hour session. Capping branch 1 is not an alternative fix; with a 23:00 closing it would cut a day worker who started at 08:00 at 22:00. **Branch 3 must not be deleted**, but its scope is narrow: a giriş **not from today** that *is* in the day window — a day session left over from a previous day, which branch 1 (today-only) and branch 2 (night-only) both miss, and which would otherwise stay open forever. **Known limit:** a genuine day shift starting before 06:00 (say 05:00) is classified as night and capped at 6 hours; the app has no opening-hour concept, so 06:00 is the best available derivation and it lives in one constant. `mesai_hatirlatma` uses the **same** day window (061's guard depended on the dropped column, and a reminder that disagreed with the closer would nag a night worker who started after midnight — defensive in practice, since that session is closed at the 6-hour mark long before the reminder's `now >= closing` threshold, but it matters if the closing cron stalls). Functions are recreated **before** the column is dropped, so no live function ever references a missing column. Smoke test PASS 41 rewritten, and **41(g) exercises the owner's scenario end to end**: a night session is auto-closed at 6 hours, the person clocks in again five minutes later, and that continuation is closed at 6 hours too rather than being dragged to the evening closing. Writing it also surfaced **two broken assertions already in this batch**, both fixed here: PASS 40(d) used a `now() - 23 hours` fixture that was correct under 060 but became a *night* session once 062 keyed the night branch on wall-clock time, so it failed at every hour of the day (now a previous-day 00:10 giriş, inside the `[00:00, 00:30)` day window that the test's 00:30 closing produces); and PASS 41(e) pinned its night fixture at a literal `00:35` while asserting "6 hours have not elapsed, must stay open", which failed whenever the suite ran after 06:35 (now `now()`, a zero-minute session that is open under any clock).
